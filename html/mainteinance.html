Maintenance

Prevent problems before they start.

Good habits can help your computer stay faster, cooler, and more reliable over time.

Clean hardware regularly

Dust buildup can cause overheating and reduce performance. Clean vents and fans carefully.

Keep software updated

Updates often fix security issues and improve stability. Install them when available.

Use antivirus protection

Protect your system from malware that may slow down your computer or cause strange errors.

Back up important files

A backup helps you recover data if a drive fails or the system crashes unexpectedly.

Manage startup apps

Too many apps opening when Windows starts will slow down your PC. Disable apps you don't use every day using Task Manager.

Free up disk space

A drive that is nearly full slows down everything. Periodically delete temporary files, empty your Recycle Bin, and remove large unused files.

Maintain good airflow

Keep your computer off thick rugs or soft carpets and leave several inches of open space around vents so hot air can escape easily.

Restart your PC regularly

Leaving your computer on for weeks can cause memory slowdowns. Restarting once a week clears memory cache and completes system updates.

Check storage drive health

Storage drives wear down with age. Check drive status with system tools or manufacturer software so you can replace a failing drive early.

Use a surge protector

Sudden power spikes and electrical storms can fry internal parts. Plug your computer into a surge-protected power strip instead of a bare wall socket.

Clean keyboard and mouse

Dust, food crumbs, and oils cause sticky keys and erratic mouse tracking. Gently wipe down your keyboard, mouse, and screen with microfiber cloths.

Organize cables and ports

Tightly pulled or bent cables can break internal wires and damage USB or power ports. Keep cables gently curved and well organized.

Remove unused programs

Unused apps and browser extensions clutter your system and waste resources. Uninstall software and tools you no longer need.

Care for laptop batteries

Avoid leaving laptops in hot cars or constantly charging in extreme heat. If your laptop supports battery limit charging (e.g. 80%), turn it on.

Refresh thermal paste

After a few years, thermal paste on your processor dries up, causing fans to spin louder. Replacing it restores optimal cooling performance.

Keep liquids and food away

Liquid spills are the number one cause of irreversible computer damage. Keep mugs, glasses, and snacks at a safe distance from your setup.
